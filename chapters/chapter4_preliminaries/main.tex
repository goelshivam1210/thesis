\chapter{Formalisms for Open Worlds}
\label{ch:foundations}

This chapter develops the formal vocabulary used by the technical contributions.
\Cref{ch:novelty_open_world} introduced novelty as agent-relative mismatch and identified the requirements imposed by open-world autonomy; \cref{ch:related_work} positioned those requirements within the broader literature.
We now define the agent--environment framework, formalize novelty and its taxonomies, and introduce the structured abstractions that connect the dissertation's contributions.

We begin by formalizing open-world environments, the agent, and the crafting running example used throughout the dissertation (\cref{sec:open_world_environments}).
We then define the planning and learning machinery used throughout, including symbolic planning, reinforcement learning, and integrated planning tasks (\cref{sec:agent_framework}).
Next, we formalize novelty as agent-relative mismatch and present the task-impact and domain-element taxonomies (\cref{sec:novelty}).
Finally, we introduce structured abstractions as the mechanism through which agents can localize mismatch, preserve reusable knowledge, and generalize across variation (\cref{sec:abstractions}).
