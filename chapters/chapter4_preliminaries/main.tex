\chapter[Formal Preliminaries and Structured Abstractions]{Formal Preliminaries and Structured Abstractions\footnotemark}
\label{ch:foundations}
\footnotetext{Portions of this chapter are based on: S.~Goel, P.~Lymperopoulos, R.~Thielstrom, E.~Krause, P.~Feeney, P.~Lorang, S.~Schneider, Y.~Wei, E.~Kildebeck, S.~Goss, M.~C.~Hughes, L.~Liu, J.~Sinapov, and M.~Scheutz, ``A Neurosymbolic Cognitive Architecture Framework for Handling Novelties in Open Worlds,'' \textit{Artificial Intelligence}, vol.~331, p.~104111, 2024.}

This chapter develops the formal vocabulary used by the technical contributions.
\Cref{ch:novelty_open_world} introduced novelty as agent-relative mismatch and identified the requirements imposed by open-world autonomy; \cref{ch:related_work} positioned those requirements within the broader literature.
We now define the agent--environment framework, symbolic and subsymbolic representations, hybrid planning and learning, and the structured abstractions that connect the dissertation's contributions.

We begin by formalizing open-world environments and the relationship between environment states and agent representations (\cref{sec:open_world_environments}).
We then define the planning and learning machinery used throughout the dissertation, including symbolic planning, reinforcement learning, and integrated planning tasks (\cref{sec:agent_framework}).
Finally, we introduce structured abstractions as the mechanism through which agents can localize mismatch, preserve reusable knowledge, and generalize across variation (\cref{sec:abstractions}).
