\chapter{Literature Review: Adaptation Under Novelty}
\label{ch:related_work}

This chapter situates the dissertation within the broader literature on learning, planning, and adaptation under novelty.
The previous chapter argued that open-world autonomy requires more than robustness within a fixed representation: agents must sometimes revise the vocabulary, operators, or abstractions through which they understand the world.
Here we use that distinction to organize existing work.

The chapter first reviews methods that adapt within a fixed representation, including novelty detection, concept drift, continual learning, transfer learning, domain randomization, and plan repair.
It then turns to approaches that extend an agent's representation in response to failure, including open-world architectures, creative problem solving, action discovery, and symbolic-neural systems.
Finally, it considers foundation models as a source of broad priors and candidate structure, while arguing that they do not remove the need for localizable, testable, and grounded mechanisms for adaptation.
The chapter closes by identifying the gap addressed by this dissertation: structural adaptation and generalization mediated by structured abstractions.
