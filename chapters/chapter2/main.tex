\chapter{Novelty in Open-World Autonomy}
\label{ch:novelty_open_world}
\label{ch:perspectives}

This chapter defines the open-world problem setting that motivates the dissertation.
We begin by contrasting closed-world assumptions with open-world autonomy, where an agent cannot assume that its knowledge and representational vocabulary are complete with respect to the environment.
We then develop the idea of novelty as an agent-relative mismatch, drawing briefly on perspectives from psychology, neuroscience, predictive processing, and artificial intelligence to explain why novelty depends on the experience, knowledge, and representations an agent brings to the world.

With this conceptual view in place, we use the distinction between assimilation and accommodation to explain why some changes can be handled within an existing representation while others require representational revision.
The chapter closes by identifying four requirements for open-world autonomy: detecting and characterizing mismatch, adapting from limited experience, revising representations when needed, and integrating perception, reasoning, learning, and action.
